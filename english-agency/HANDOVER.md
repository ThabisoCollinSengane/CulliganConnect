## 1. What the business is
A Durban-based agency that supplies vetted, supervised, **employed** online English tutors to **adult
learners** at schools, universities, training providers and companies abroad. Run like a call-centre
operation: each client account is a "campaign", with a defined schedule, student level, KPIs
(attendance, lesson quality, satisfaction) and reporting.

Not a marketplace side-hustle. Marketplaces (Cambly, Preply, italki, Native Camp) are used only to
learn the craft. Their terms don't permit agency-controlled accounts (see R1).

## 2. Target market
- **Primary: China (adults only).** University students, adult learners, corporate training. Buyers
  are universities, adult training providers and corporate HR/L&D.
- **Parallel: Southeast Asia.** Private language centres and corporate clients in Vietnam and
  Thailand. Price-sensitive; competes with low-cost Filipino tutor supply.
- **Hard exclusion: China K-12.** Under the 2021 "Double Reduction" policy, foreign teachers based
  overseas cannot legally tutor compulsory-education-age students in mainland China. Decline and
  log any China K-12 lead. Treat it as a **BLOCKER** until an attorney confirms otherwise in writing.
- **Time zones:** SA is 6 h behind China. After-work corporate training in China (18:00–21:00) falls at
  12:00–15:00 SAST. *(Claude note: daytime in-office training in China, 09:00–18:00, falls at
  03:00–12:00 SAST. See CHALLENGE §1.)*

## 3. Delivery model
Hub-based, employed tutors. Not freelancers or contractors.
- 5 days × 8 hours = 40 ordinary hours/week ≈ 173 hours/month per tutor (below the BCEA 45-hour ceiling).
- Not 45 hours. Admin, QA and breaks would make 45 teaching hours non-compliant.
- Plan utilisation at 70–80% billable. 100% is impossible, both legally and operationally.
- Dual internet, UPS/inverter backup, noise-cancelling headsets and acoustic separation. Reliable
  power and audio is the real differentiator, not tutor accent.

Loaded cost per tutor: R17,500–R18,500/month (UIF, SDL, COIDA, leave provision, bonus).
Cost per billable hour at 70% utilisation: ~R140–145. *(Claude check: CHALLENGE §1 gets ~R152.)*
**Rule:** raise base salary only when the blended client rate sustains US$16+ for two consecutive
months. Never raise pay in anticipation of future rates.

## 5. Client pricing (recommended, pending validation)
**Do not open at US$10/hour.** At R140–145 cost per billable hour, US$10 (R180) gives only ~20–21%
gross margin.
Open at **US$12–14**, with a written escalation schedule in every contract:

| Period | Rate |
|---|---|
| Months 1–6 | US$12–14 per tutor-hour |
| Months 7–12 | US$15 |
| Year 2 | US$18 |
| Year 3+ | Annual increase of the greater of CPI + 3% or 5% |

US$12 ≈ 34% gross margin; US$14 ≈ 44%. US$10 only for a short, defined paid pilot with a documented step-up.
**Governing metric:** client rate ÷ tutor loaded cost per billable hour must stay above ~2.0–2.5×.
Break-even illustration (placeholder): R60,000/month fixed ÷ ~R74–110 margin/hr ≈ 550–810 billable
hours/month ≈ 5–7 tutors at 70% utilisation.

## 6. Client acquisition (the real bottleneck)
A website does not generate clients. It supports a sale that is already in progress.
Buyers: HR/L&D managers, training coordinators, team leads with budget, SME founders/office managers.
SMEs are the best first target because decision chains are short.

Channels, ranked:
1. LinkedIn outreach to HR/L&D at internationally-operating companies in China and Vietnam. Named
   outcome, not generic introduction. *(Claude note: LinkedIn's China reach is limited. See CHALLENGE §3.)*
2. Chambers of commerce / bilateral trade associations, for warm introductions.
3. Paid 90-minute pilot workshop. This filters out non-serious buyers and produces the first case study. **Never free.**
4. Tender platforms (VNEPS, Sell to State, TendersGo). Slower, but one institutional contract anchors the business. Not before 1–2 paying corporate clients.
5. Language-school subcontracting. Lower margin, but gives a reference.

The owner's 45 hours/week go into sales, not teaching. The first hire is a tutor, so the director can sell.
