## First-pass analysis (written before the adults-only decision — kept for the record)

> ### ⚠️ Biggest risk found: China's school-age market is legally closed to tutors based in South Africa
> China's 2021 "Double Reduction" rules ban foreign teachers **physically located outside mainland
> China** from teaching students in compulsory education (roughly ages 6–15), and cover foreign
> languages, i.e. English. VIPKid and 51Talk shut their foreign-teacher K-12 programmes because of it.
> I found **no 2025/2026 official source saying this has been reversed** — most current guides (e.g.
> TEFL Institute, Mar 2026) still say overseas-based teachers can't legally teach school-age children
> in the mainland online. A Chinese school "agreeing" to a contract doesn't make it legal, and payment
> and enforcement against a Chinese counterparty would be weak.
> **What remains possible in China:** adult learners, business English and exam prep (IELTS/TOEFL),
> through a *licensed Chinese company*. That's a partner deal, not "schools", and it's lower priority.
> **Recommendation:** don't make China K-12 schools the launch market. Lead with Thailand and Vietnam,
> then Japan and Korea.

## Summary
- Demand for remote native-English teaching into Asian **schools** is real and proven. Braincloud
  Learning (Thailand) delivers online native-speaker teachers into Grades 1–6 classrooms. Inagi City
  (Tokyo) runs online 1:1 conversation for all Year-2 junior-high students via the Philippines.
- South Africa is commonly listed as a "native English-speaking" country in Thai, Chinese and Korean
  hiring rules. Philippine suppliers often don't get that status, and that's the SA edge.
- The time zones work as a call-centre night/morning shift. The Asian school day runs from about
  01:30 to 11:00 SAST.
- Many schools in Vietnam and Thailand don't hire foreign teachers directly. They buy through
  intermediaries (language centres, edtech companies). **Sell to the intermediaries first.**

## Time-zone fit (stable facts: SAST = UTC+2, no daylight saving)
| Market | UTC offset | Ahead of SA | School day 08:00–16:00 local = SA time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thailand, Vietnam | +7 | 5 h | 03:00–11:00 |
| China, Philippines, Taiwan | +8 | 6 h | 02:00–10:00 |
| Japan, Korea | +9 | 7 h | 01:00–09:00 |
Implication: shifts run roughly **01:00–11:00 SAST**. Adult/after-school demand (18:00–21:00 local)
falls at about 11:00–16:00 SAST. A two-shift roster covers both, which is a normal BPO pattern.

## Who the agency actually competes with
Philippine BPO-style tutoring companies (QQEnglish ~1,300 teachers, Cebu; Native Camp; RareJob) serve
Japan and Korea at very low prices. Consumer lessons run from about ¥400 per lesson
(https://www.english-dialogclub.com/blog/?p=7652, LOW). **Don't compete on price.** Compete on:
1. "Native English" status, where buyers or regulations ask for it (Thailand, China in-country, Korea).
2. Call-centre-grade reliability: cover tutors, power backup, QA scoring and reporting.
3. Classroom-format delivery, i.e. one tutor on screen with a whole class plus a local teacher (the Braincloud model), not just 1:1.
