## 0. Rules for Claude Code (read first)

1. **Evidence over confidence.** Every rate, price, rule or claim you report must have a source URL and the date you checked it. If you can't verify it, label it `UNVERIFIED`. Never invent rates, market sizes, or legal requirements.

## 2. The vision (owner's plan, summarised)

**Stage A — Learn the craft.** Get a TEFL certificate. Teach online on existing platforms to learn how lessons work, what students want, what the platforms feel like from the inside.

**Stage B — Build the agency.** Register a company. Rent a large empty room in Durban, fit it out as a call-centre-style hub with many computers, and employ people to teach online. Instead of call-centre "campaigns", the agency's "campaigns" are **client accounts**: schools or organisations in specific countries/regions that need English teachers. The owner will go out and find that work the way call-centre businesses win contracts. The owner's view: finding suitable staff will be easy.

**Run as a legitimate business:** registered company, contracts, proper payroll/tax, compliance.

## 3. Honest assessment — assumptions to test (do not skip)

These are the places the plan is most likely to break. Research tasks in section 8 are designed to test them.

| # | Risk | Why it matters | What to find out |
|---|------|----------------|------------------|
| A | **Marketplaces are not the engine.** Cambly, Preply, italki, Native Camp are built for *individual* tutors with their own verified accounts and payouts. Running many tutors through accounts the agency controls very likely breaks their terms. | The marketplaces are for *learning the craft and market research*. They cannot be the business. | R1: check each platform's terms on agencies, account sharing, subcontracting. |
| B | **Demand is the hard part, not supply.** Finding tutors in Durban is probably easy. Winning and keeping paying schools/companies abroad is the real business. | A hub with no signed clients is just rent. | R2, R3, R4: who buys, what they pay, how they choose providers. |
| C | **Capex before demand.** Renting a big room and buying many PCs before any client commitment is the biggest financial risk. | Cash burn with no revenue. | Staged roadmap (section 4) with gates. |
| D | **Employees vs independent contractors.** In South Africa, "contractors" who work set hours for one client may legally be treated as employees (UIF, leave, BCEA hours, dismissal rules). | Misclassification = penalties and disputes. | R6. |
| E | **Getting paid from abroad.** Client payment terms, forex, fees, invoicing, exchange-control rules, non-paying foreign clients. | Revenue risk and margin erosion. | R7, R13. |
| F | **Load-shedding and connectivity.** A lesson that drops mid-class damages the client relationship. | The hub's real selling point is reliability (power + dual internet + supervision + cover tutors). | R8. |
| G | **Noise and audio.** Many tutors talking in one room, all on live audio. | Poor audio quality = lost clients. | R8 (acoustic booths/partitions, noise-cancelling headsets). |
| H | **Desktops vs laptops.** PCs die instantly in an outage without UPS. Laptops have built-in battery ride-through, lower power draw, and let the model flex to hybrid/remote. | Claude's recommendation to test: cost refurbished business laptops against desktops+UPS. | R8. |
| I | **Child safeguarding and student data** (if teaching minors). POPIA, background checks, lesson recording policy. | Legal and reputational risk. | R6. |
| J | **Rates are unknown.** We know what individual tutors earn on marketplaces. We do **not** yet know what schools/companies pay an agency per tutor-hour. | The whole model depends on it. | R4. |

**Owner's core insight to keep:** the owner already understands operations (shifts, QA, team leaders). That is a genuine advantage. The gap is sales and client acquisition.

## 4. Roadmap with gates (proposed — owner to confirm)

**Phase 0 — Learn the craft (now → ~3 months)**
- Get an Ofqual-regulated 120-hour TEFL (see section 5.2).
- Start on Cambly and italki (no cert needed to start), add Preply once certified.
- Log everything: hours taught, no-show rate, which time slots fill, what students ask for, what makes a lesson good or bad, load-shedding incidents.
- **Gate 1 → Phase 1:** TEFL obtained + roughly 100 taught hours + written lessons-learned notes.

**Phase 1 — Validate demand (overlaps Phase 0)**
- Run research tasks R1–R4 and R13.
- Build a target list of regions and prospective clients.
- Launch a simple landing page (see section 7) to test interest.
- **Gate 2 → Phase 2:** at least one written commitment (letter of intent or paid pilot) from a school/company for a defined number of weekly tutor-hours, with payment terms understood.

**Phase 2 — Pilot (remote, small)**
- 2–5 tutors working from their own locations (or the owner's space), 1–2 clients, 8+ weeks.
- Measure: attendance, client satisfaction, tutor utilisation, margin per hour, incident log.
- Register the company when the first paid pilot is confirmed (R6 sets out the steps).

**Phase 3 — Formalise**
- Company registered, business bank account, tax registrations, contracts, POPIA basics, tutor agreements.

**Phase 4 — Hub (only if the numbers justify it)**
- **Gate 3:** pilot margins positive for 2 consecutive months AND committed client hours cover the hub's break-even (see section 6).
- Then rent the room, fit out, hire in batches (e.g. 5 → 10 → 20 seats), not all at once.

## 6. Draft business model and unit economics

**Service (draft):** managed online English teaching/conversation staffing for schools and organisations. The agency supplies vetted, trained, supervised tutors on a schedule, with replacement cover and quality reporting.

**"Campaign" equivalent:** a client account with a defined schedule (hours/week), student level, curriculum or conversation format, and KPIs (attendance, lesson quality score, client satisfaction).

**Roles (eventual):** owner/operations lead, team leaders (QA and scheduling), tutors, recruiter/trainer, sales.

**Formula**
```
Revenue per tutor-hour  = client rate (USD) × FX × (1 − payment/forex fees)
Direct cost per hour    = tutor pay + employer costs (UIF/SDL/COIDA etc. — R6 to confirm)
Gross margin per hour   = revenue − direct cost
Break-even billable hrs = monthly fixed costs ÷ gross margin per hour
```
Fixed costs: rent, power backup, fibre (dual), equipment depreciation, software, admin, insurance, accounting.
Adjust for **utilisation** (planned hours that actually get billed — assume 60–80% until measured).

**Illustration only — placeholders, NOT market data**

| Client rate | In ZAR (R18/$) | Tutor pay (placeholder) | Gross margin/hr | Margin % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $8 | R144 | R100 | R44 | 31% |
| $10 | R180 | R100 | R80 | 44% |
| $12 | R216 | R100 | R116 | 54% |

If fixed costs were R60,000/month (**placeholder, not a quote**) and margin were R80/hr, break-even is 750 billable hours/month (≈ 34 hrs/day over 22 days, or roughly 7 tutors billing 5 hrs/day). Real figures come from R4 (client rates), R8 (hub costs) and R9 (tutor pay).

## 7. Website — build specification (Claude Code)

**Purpose:** (1) win B2B clients (schools/companies), (2) recruit tutors, (3) look legitimate and trustworthy.

**Do not start building until the owner confirms:** working name, target regions for launch, and the stack. Suggested default (confirm first): Next.js on Vercel + Supabase for forms, storage and admin.

**MVP pages**
1. **Home** — what we do, who it's for, trust signals, clear call to action.
2. **For Schools & Organisations** — service explanation, how it works (scope → trial lesson → schedule → reporting), reliability promises we can actually keep, "Request a quote / book a call".
3. **For Tutors** — requirements, process, pay model (only publish what the owner approves), application form.
4. **About / Trust** — who runs it, standards, safeguarding, quality process.
5. **Contact** — form + booking link.
6. **Legal** — Privacy Policy (POPIA-aware), Terms, cookie notice.

**MVP features**
- Lead form → stored in database + email notification.
- Tutor application form: CV upload, intro-video link, TEFL certificate upload, availability, internet/power setup questions. Store securely; applicants' personal data is sensitive.
- Simple admin view (login-protected) for leads and applications.
- Booking embed (e.g. Calendly-type) for discovery calls.

**Non-functional**
- Mobile-first; fast on slow connections (clients and tutors may be on phones).
- SEO basics for B2B search terms.
- Privacy-friendly analytics; consent where required.
- Accessibility basics.
- Content built so a second language can be added later (target-region languages: research R2).

**Phase 2 internal tools (later, after a paid pilot):** tutor roster, scheduling/calendar, attendance and hours tracking, payroll export, lesson QA scoring, client reporting dashboard.

**Content rule:** no testimonials, statistics or credentials on the site that aren't real and documented.
