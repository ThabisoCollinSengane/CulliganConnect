# Sales playbook + viability verdict: Clearline English
**Date:** 2026-11-08 · **Author:** Claude (Chief of Staff) · **Status:** recommendation. Nothing here has been sent or spent.

---

## 1. Is this a viable business? Straight answer
**Yes, as a small, profitable, sales-led service business. No, in the original form.**

| Version | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| US$12 1:1 lessons, salaried tutors, office hub first, China schoolchildren | **Not viable** | Loses money even at 70% utilisation (calculator); China K-12 is illegal for overseas tutors; rent before revenue |
| **Solo → small team, small-group corporate programmes + subcontract capacity, Vietnam/Thailand first, prepaid with minimum hours** | **Viable if you can sell** | ~R30k/month solo once ~70 teaching hrs/month are sold; a 5-tutor remote team clears ~R45k–R120k/month profit at 50–70% utilisation *after* paying you R35k (calculator; prices are hypotheses) |

**Your real weaknesses (buyers will notice them):**
1. No track record, no company, no clients, no certificate yet. You're asking strangers abroad to trust a new supplier.
2. You're far away and unknown. Local in-person trainers and Filipino online providers are already there.
3. Long sales cycles at big firms (FPT, Teleperformance) mean months, not weeks.

**Your real strengths (lead with these):**
1. **You've worked the floor of UK/US contact centres.** You know QA scorecards, CSAT, escalations and shift cover. No language school can say that to a BPO.
2. South Africa is commonly treated as a native-English country (MED-LOW evidence, see R2).
3. You can run delivery like a campaign: cover tutors, backup power and internet, reporting.
4. Price: online small groups cost a company less than in-person trainers they already pay US$21–38/h for (job-ad evidence, R4).

**The make-or-break test (set it now):**
> **Within 90 days of starting outreach and ~150 personalised contacts: at least 2 paid pilots and 1 recurring client.**
> If you hit it, keep going and plan the first hire. If you get zero pilots, change the segment, offer or price. **Don't** spend on an office, a website or a company yet.

## 2. Fix the credibility gap before the first message (weeks 0–2)
You only get one first impression. Have these ready, all honest:

| Asset | What it is | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **TEFL in progress** (TEFL Academy L3 + Business English module) | Vietnam *requires* TEFL/TESOL/CELTA for foreign English teachers; every buyer will ask | ≈R2.3k (**your approval**) |
| **LinkedIn profile rewrite** | Headline: "Founder, Clearline English · Online English for contact-centre & corporate teams · ex-UK/US contact-centre agent". Add your campaign experience | Free |
| **2-minute intro video** (phone camera, good headset) | You, clear audio, explaining the offer. It *proves* your accent and audio quality | Free |
| **One-page capability sheet (PDF)** | What you do, who for, how a pilot works, price, how you guarantee reliability. No fake logos or stats | Free (AI team can draft) |
| **Sample 60-min lesson plan + sample progress report** | Shows the "managed" part buyers pay extra for | Free (AI team can draft) |
| **A way to get paid** | Invoice in US$ and receive via Wise Business/Payoneer (R7 not yet researched); ask the accountant whether you can invoice as a sole proprietor before registering the Pty Ltd | Accountant call |
| **Pilot agreement (1 page)** | Scope, date, US$150, payment upfront, credit if they continue | Attorney review |

Don't claim certificates, clients, team size or results you don't have. "Founder, launching with pilot clients" is fine and honest.

## 3. How to approach each type of contact

### A. BPO / call-centre founders (THIENTU, Innovature): your warmest door
**Channel:** phone or WhatsApp first (their published numbers), then email. BPO founders pick up phones.
**Talk their language:** QA scores, CSAT, first-call resolution, escalations, client complaints about accent or clarity. Not grammar.
**30-second opener:**
> "Hi, this is Thabiso from Clearline English in South Africa. I spent five years taking calls on UK and US campaigns, and now I coach agents in the English that moves QA scores: call control, empathy, clear explanations. You sell English-speaking teams, so I wanted to ask: where do your clients push back most on agent English?"
**Then listen.** Their answer is your pilot.
**Offer:** a 90-minute paid workshop (US$150) for up to 12 agents on *their* worst QA item, credited if they continue. Before and after, use **their own QA scorecard**: agree two or three items to measure.
**Data rule:** don't ask for call recordings with customer data. Ask for anonymised scenarios or their QA criteria instead.

### B. Training providers (Axcela, E&G, KTDC, Modulo, Globish): sell capacity, not competition
**Channel:** email or contact form, then a phone follow-up two days later. LinkedIn to the named founder or academic director.
**Their pain:** finding reliable native-speaker trainers for evening slots, short-notice cover, remote clients, scaling online.
**Opener:**
> "I run an online trainer team from South Africa, set up like a contact centre: cover trainers, backup power and internet, lesson QA. If you ever have corporate groups you can't staff, especially evenings or online, we can deliver under your brand and curriculum."
**Trust builders:** offer a **paid demo lesson** with one of their groups, or a recorded demo they can review. Put a **non-solicit clause** in writing: you'll never approach their clients directly. Providers only subcontract to people who won't steal clients.
**Price:** US$16–20/h with a monthly minimum. Don't go below your US$14 floor.

### C. IT and corporate L&D (NashTech, TMA, KMS)
**Channel:** LinkedIn to the named director or the L&D/HR manager (title search), with a short note. Then email via the form.
**Hook:** "Your engineers talk to UK/US clients every day. We run 4–8-person online groups on stand-ups, client calls and email."
**Expect:** a slower cycle (procurement). Treat these as month 2–4 revenue, not week 2.

### D. Chambers (AmCham, BritCham, EuroCham, AMCHAM Thailand): borrow their trust
**Ask for 15 minutes**, not membership: "How do training suppliers usually get in front of members? Events, committees, member offers?"
**Give something:** offer a **45-minute online talk for members**, e.g. "Running clear calls with UK and US clients". This is marketing, not a pilot, so it's your call whether to do it free. Attendees become warm leads.
**Membership or event fees need your approval.** Don't join until you've had at least one conversation that shows members would buy.

## 4. The outreach rhythm (call-centre discipline)
| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | Research 5 min per lead: one specific hook (news, job ad, client, LinkedIn post) |
| Day 0 | First touch: phone/WhatsApp (BPOs) or personalised email/form (others) |
| Day 2 | LinkedIn connection note (≤300 chars) to the named person |
| Day 4 | Follow-up 1: add something useful (e.g. "3 phrases that cut escalations", your intro video) |
| Day 10 | Follow-up 2: short, "Shall I close this off?" |
| Then | Stop. Move them to "nurture"; re-contact in 90 days |
**Volume:** 5 new leads/day → 25/week. **Track:** contacted → replied → call → pilot → paying (tracker `status`).
**Targets to aim for (not data, measure them):** ~10–20% reply → ~1 call per 10 contacts → ~1 pilot per 3 calls.

## 5. Discovery call (15–20 min) structure
1. **Their world (5 min):** "Who needs English most, and for what situations?" "What have you tried?" "What does bad English cost you: complaints, lost deals, QA fails?"
2. **Fit check (3 min):** headcount, levels, schedule/time zone, decision-maker, budget owner.
3. **Your proposal (5 min):** the US$150 pilot on their #1 problem, plus what happens after (12-week group programme, US$32/group-hour, prepaid monthly, progress reports).
4. **Close (2 min):** "Can we put a date in for the pilot? I'll send a one-page agreement and the invoice today."

## 6. Objections and honest answers
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We already have a provider." | "Good. Many clients use us for evening groups, cover or teams the main provider can't staff. Want to test us on one group?" |
| "Too expensive." | "Per learner it's about US$4–8 an hour in a group of 6, less than an in-person trainer. The pilot is US$150 to prove it first." |
| "You're in South Africa?" | "Yes. English is a first language here and South Africa is commonly accepted as a native-English country. You get a team run like a contact centre, with backup power, backup internet and cover trainers." |
| "Are you certified?" | Truthfully: "I'm completing a 120-hour Ofqual-regulated TEFL with a Business English module" (or "I hold…" once done). |
| "Send me information." | "Of course. I'll send a one-page summary now. Can we book 15 minutes Thursday to see if it fits?" |
| "Can you do kids/schools?" | "We focus on adult and workplace English." (China K-12 is a hard no.) |

## 7. How you *keep* clients (where the profit is)
- Start every group with a short placement check, and send a **monthly one-page progress report** to the buyer.
- **Never miss a lesson:** backup power and internet at home from day one, plus a named cover tutor once you hire.
- After 4 weeks, ask for a written comment you can quote **with permission**. That's your first real case study.
- Upsell: a second group, executive 1:1, a new department.
